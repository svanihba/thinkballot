Methodology

How we stay neutral

ThinkBallot never tells you how to vote. These are the rules every page follows.

The rules

  1. Official sources only. Everything comes from the California Secretary of State's Official Voter Information Guide and the Legislative Analyst's Office. No news, no social media.
  2. Their words, not ours. Summaries, YES and NO meanings, fiscal effects and arguments are shown word for word, with a link to the exact page. We do not use AI to write them.
  3. Both sides, equally. YES always comes before NO, in the same layout, style and colour. The arguments for and against sit side by side.
  4. If it isn't in the source, we don't show it. A missing field says "Not available in the official guide" instead of being filled in.
  5. Your privacy. We use only the ZIP code from your address to find your likely districts. Addresses and questions are never stored or logged. Stars and reading progress stay in your own browser.

Bias Buster

Bias Buster is the part of ThinkBallot that checks every piece of text we write and every AI answer before you see it. It has two jobs: no bias and no made-up facts.

  1. Quote check. Every point in an answer must quote the official text, and the quote must be found there word for word. If it isn't, the point is removed.
  2. Number check. Every number, dollar amount, percentage or date must appear in the official text.
  3. Loaded-word check. Persuasive words like "disastrous" or "common-sense" are caught unless the official text itself uses them.
  4. Balance check. When both sides are covered, YES and NO must both be there, at similar length.
  5. AI review. A second AI model reviews each answer for one-sided framing, hints about how to vote, and claims the source doesn't support. Its edits go through checks 1 to 4 again. If the review can't run, the answer is held back.

Before any data goes live, Bias Buster also confirms that every official field we stored appears word for word on its source page, which catches copying mistakes.
