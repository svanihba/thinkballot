Methodology

How we stay neutral

ThinkBallot never tells you how to vote. Neutrality isn't a promise we make; it's how the system is built. Here's the whole pipeline, in plain terms.

The pipeline

1 · Official sources

The Secretary of State's Official Voter Information Guide and the Legislative Analyst's Office. Nothing else.

2 · Copy, word for word

Each field is copied exactly, with a link to the page it came from. No AI writes the cards.

3 · Bias Buster verifies

Every field must be found word for word on its source page, or nothing is published. Our own labels must pass every check.

4 · Your ballot

What you read is a fast lookup over that checked data. No AI runs while you browse.

One exception: when you ask a question, an AI drafts an answer from the official text only, with quotes, and Bias Buster checks it before you see it.

Your question

"How should I vote?" gets a fixed answer: that's your call.

The rules

RuleWhat it means
Official sources onlyEverything comes from the official voter guide and the Legislative Analyst. No news, no social media.

Bias Buster

Bias Buster checks every piece of text we write and every AI answer before you see it. Two jobs: no bias and no made-up facts.

  1. Quote check. Every point in an answer must quote the official text, and the quote must be found there word for word. If it isn't, the point is removed.
  2. Number check. Every number, dollar amount, percentage or date must appear in the official text.
  3. Loaded-word check. Persuasive words like "disastrous" or "common-sense" are caught unless the official text itself uses them.
  4. Balance check. When both sides are covered, YES and NO must both be there, at similar length.
  5. AI review. A second AI model reviews each answer for one-sided framing, hints about how to vote, and claims the source doesn't support. Its edits go through checks 1 to 4 again. If the review can't run, the answer is held back.

Community rules

{% if not community_open %}

Community discussion opens after the election. These are the rules it will follow.

{% endif %}

The discussion and community facts sit in a separate zone under each official card. They can never change the official card. People may be partisan there; ThinkBallot never is. Moderation judges conduct, never viewpoint.

  1. Disagree with ideas, not people. No insults, harassment or slurs.
  2. No spam, ads or links to unofficial sources passed off as facts.
  3. Don't share anyone's personal information, including your own.
  4. Comments appear right away. If five established accounts flag one, it's hidden until a moderator reviews it.
  5. Community facts need a source link and neutral wording (checked by Bias Buster). Government sources are marked ✓ official source. They're published only after people who lean YES and people who lean NO both rate them helpful (bridging consensus), and a moderator confirms the citation supports them.

What we will never do

No endorsements. No predictions. No "how you should vote."
